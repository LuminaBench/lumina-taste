Pricing a seat

Riya Mehta stopped charging by the seat and started charging for reports people opened. Invoices got smaller. Revenue went up 23%.

RMRiya MehtaHead of Finance, Moss Studio

Pricing a seatEpisode 12 · Halyard Radio
0:00 / 32:14

Chapters

Transcript

0:00AnaMost of us price by the seat because everyone else does. Riya, you stopped. What made you look at it in the first place?

0:41RiyaA customer with four people was running sixty reports a day, and a customer with ninety people ran two a week. They paid us very different amounts, and the wrong way round. Seats were measuring the size of the company, not how much they got from us.

3:48AnaSo what did you charge for instead?

3:55RiyaReports that were opened. Not created, opened. If nobody reads it, we did not do anything for you. It took three weeks of arguing to get to a unit that simple.

10:12AnaThere is a month in your numbers where invoices go down and revenue goes up. Explain that one.

10:30RiyaThe average invoice fell by 11% because small teams paid less. But 140 teams that had been stuck on the free plan started paying, because the first price was now £9 and not £120. More customers, smaller invoices, more money.

18:25AnaHow did you tell the people who were already paying?

18:40RiyaOne email, six sentences, with their own number in it: what you paid last month, what you would have paid on the new plan. For 82% of them the second number was lower. Nobody who writes to complain has read a pricing page, so we put the answer in the email.

26:00AnaWhat would you do differently?

26:08RiyaMove sooner. We spent a year improving a seat price that was never going to fit. And I would have kept the old plan for anyone who asked. Nine customers asked. It would have cost us nothing.
